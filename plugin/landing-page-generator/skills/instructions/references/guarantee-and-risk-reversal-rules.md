# Guarantee And Risk Reversal Rules

## Purpose
Reduce buyer hesitation without making claims that are unsafe or legally problematic.

## Types Of Guarantees
- Refund guarantee
- Satisfaction guarantee
- Service-level guarantee
- Replacement guarantee
- Revision guarantee
- Delivery-time guarantee

## When To Use Each
- Refund guarantee: when refund terms are real and enforceable
- Satisfaction guarantee: when the service can be revised to match scope
- Service-level guarantee: when a metric or response time can be controlled
- Replacement guarantee: for products with replacement policies
- Revision guarantee: for creative or consulting work

## When Not To Use Guarantees
- When the business cannot reliably honor them
- When the offer outcome depends heavily on the buyer's execution
- When a guarantee would violate policy, law, or contract terms

## Risk Reversal Without A Formal Guarantee
- Clear scope
- Clear revision policy
- Clear onboarding
- Clear support expectations
- Clear cancellation terms

## Safe Placeholder Wording
- `[Insert approved guarantee language]`
- `[Insert refund policy summary]`
- `[Insert revision policy]`

## Unsafe Wording Examples
- "Guaranteed to double your sales."
- "100 percent risk free" if not true
- "No questions asked" if the policy does not say that

## Legal Disclaimer Reminder
- Final guarantee language should be reviewed by a qualified professional when needed.
- This guide is not legal advice.

## Guarantee Section Templates
### Refund Style
"If the service does not match the agreed scope, we will work with you on revisions or refunds according to the published policy."

### Satisfaction Style
"If the final deliverable is not aligned with the brief, we will revise it until it is."

### Service-Level Style
"We commit to delivering the first draft within [timeframe], assuming the required inputs are provided on time."

### Product Style
"If the product arrives damaged, contact us for replacement according to policy."

## Cautions For Regulated Industries
- Keep the guarantee narrow and factual.
- Avoid implying performance or medical outcomes.
- Avoid financial outcome promises.

## Final Rule
Risk reversal should lower friction, not create legal or ethical risk.
