# Conversion Copy Principles

## PAS
### What It Is
Problem, Agitation, Solution.
### When To Use
Best for pages with a clear pain and clear relief.
### Example
"Your page is not converting. Every unclear section costs clicks. Use a conversion-first structure that guides visitors to one action."
### Caution
Do not overstate the pain just to create drama.

## AIDA
### What It Is
Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.
### When To Use
Good for shorter pages and headline-driven layouts.
### Example
Attention headline, interest-building subheadline, desire via proof and benefits, action via CTA.
### Caution
Weak if the page lacks proof or a specific mechanism.

## Before-After-Bridge
### What It Is
Show current state, desired state, and the bridge between them.
### When To Use
Useful when transformation is easy to visualize.
### Example
"Before: vague page. After: focused page. Bridge: this landing page system."
### Caution
Do not promise impossible transformations.

## Jobs To Be Done
### What It Is
People hire products to make progress in their lives or work.
### When To Use
Useful for reframing features into outcomes.
### Example
"Hire this page to turn traffic into qualified calls."
### Caution
Do not describe the job too abstractly.

## Voice Of Customer Language
### What It Is
The exact words prospects use to describe pain, outcomes, and objections.
### When To Use
Always when available.
### Example
"I need something that looks credible without feeling salesy."
### Caution
Avoid polishing away the customer's real wording.

## Specificity
### What It Is
Concrete numbers, concrete process, concrete audience.
### When To Use
Always.
### Example
"A 7-section landing page for B2B SaaS."
### Caution
Specific does not mean fabricated.

## Message-Market Fit
### What It Is
The message should match the audience's state of awareness and urgency.
### When To Use
Before writing the hero.
### Example
A technical audience gets mechanism and implementation detail. A cold audience gets clearer pain and outcome.
### Caution
Do not use the same angle for every audience.

## Objection Handling
### What It Is
Answer the reasons people hesitate.
### When To Use
In FAQ, proof, and guarantee sections.
### Example
"Not sure if it will fit your brand? We can adapt tone and layout."
### Caution
Do not invent objections the audience does not have.

## Risk Reversal
### What It Is
Reduce the perceived downside of saying yes.
### When To Use
When there is a real guarantee, revision policy, or low-friction trial.
### Example
"You get revisions until the scope matches the brief."
### Caution
Never promise outcomes you cannot control.

## Value Amplification
### What It Is
Explain how the offer saves time, money, or effort.
### When To Use
In offer and pricing sections.
### Example
"One draft replaces days of scattered writing."
### Caution
Do not inflate savings with fake numbers.

## Ethical Urgency
### What It Is
Real deadlines, launch dates, or capacity limits.
### When To Use
Only when true.
### Example
"Book before Friday to be included in the next build slot."
### Caution
Do not create fake countdowns.

## Ethical Scarcity
### What It Is
Real limited availability.
### When To Use
Only when factual.
### Example
"We only take three projects per month."
### Caution
Do not pretend capacity is limited if it is not.

## CTA Psychology
### What It Is
Reduce ambiguity and make the next step feel safe.
### When To Use
Every CTA.
### Example
"See the draft" is often better than "Submit."
### Caution
Do not use aggressive CTAs when the buyer needs reassurance.

## Readability Rules
- One idea per paragraph
- Short sentences for complex offers
- Subheadings that carry meaning
- Bullets for scanability
- Plain language over jargon

## Friction Reduction
### What It Is
Remove mental and operational effort.
### Example
"No design system setup required."
### Caution
Don't hide important constraints.

## Trust Building
### What It Is
Use proof, clarity, transparency, and specificity.
### Example
"Here is what is included, what is not, and what you need to provide."

## Claim Substantiation
### What It Is
Every meaningful claim should be supported by proof, mechanism, or clear qualification.
### Rule
If it cannot be supported, make it a placeholder or remove it.
